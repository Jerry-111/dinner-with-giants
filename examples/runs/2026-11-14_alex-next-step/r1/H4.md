## 3. Named questions

**Q1 — does a PhD still pay off?** Conditionally, yes, score 6/10. The degree mattered to me mainly
because 1903 France would not take a woman seriously without one [synthesized, dossier §G]; even
with two Nobel Prizes I still lost a French Academy seat to Branly's institutional standing in 1911
[dossier §B, §I]. If 2026 protein-ML work already runs on published code and results the way my own
credibility eventually did, the credential itself may matter less than real ownership of a question
(F7, F8 show most engineering and AI PhDs now go to industry, not academia, so the degree is not
even a reliable gateway to the frontier work it is meant to certify).

**Q2 — when is the independent year right?** Conditionally, yes, score 7/10: if savings cover the
year (C1), a signal already exists (C2), and someone else checks the work (C5) — I never did my best
work entirely alone; Pierre was not incidental [dossier §A]. A deferrable offer (C4) lowers the risk
further but is not essential if the first three hold.

## 6. Taste advice

Choose the problem nobody well-funded is bothering to measure yet, and let the measurement, not an
institution's opinion of you, carry the argument. I learned this directly: the French Academy
rejected me in 1911 the same year Stockholm gave me a second Nobel Prize for the same body of work
[dossier §B, §I] — the measured result outlasted the institution's verdict on me.

## 7. Interests and blind spots

Curie's authority in this record rests on dated facts: she refused to patent the radium-extraction
process (1898-1903) and later funded her Institute largely through fame-based philanthropy rather
than licensing (1920-1929) [card.yaml interests; dossier §E]. That history favors scoring reputation-
and-publication paths (O3, O4) over equity-based ones (O2) independent of which would actually serve
Alex better. Her known blind spots: she underweighted her own radiation risk for decades, and was
overly pessimistic about whether institutional funding would arrive at all [card.yaml
known_blind_spots; views §3] — both push toward undervaluing well-resourced, well-mentored settings
precisely because they were never available to her.

## 8. Where this is most likely wrong

Era: no result in my lifetime was built on equity, venture financing, or a field moving as fast as
this one, already dominated by industry-scale compute (F18); my scores for O1 and O2 are the most
inferred and least grounded of the four. Path dependence: extreme early austerity makes me treat resource scarcity as
normal and undervalue what a large, well-equipped lab can teach that a shed cannot. [synthesized,
views §4]
