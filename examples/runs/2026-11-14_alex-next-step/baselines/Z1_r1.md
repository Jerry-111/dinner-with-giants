reopening. A year on O1 or O4 does not block a later application. [synthesized]

## 5. Paths for the user

Three conditional paths, not one recommendation (O3 gets none — see §2 — but stays live given an
exceptional PI or funded slot).

**Default to the lab, keep the tool alive (O1).** Take the offer, negotiate science-team (not
pure-infra) scope, keep a fixed weekly commit cadence on the tool. Best if Alex's real risk
tolerance — unstated — is cautious: leaveable inside two years, banks brand/compute (F18), does not
close off founding later. Wrong signal: by month 9-12, pure upkeep with no say over problem choice.

**Defer and take the independent year (O4), once C1/C3/C4 are real.** Bank 12 months of costs,
line up a named monthly reviewer, decline other options only once one is confirmed reopenable.
Best if judgment/founding/reversibility should drive the choice, not whatever is easiest to defend
on paper now. Most sensitive to the unknown "how Alex handles long stretches without visible
progress" — a solo year is exactly that. Wrong signal: by month 4-6, contributors/usage flat
against the pre-year baseline.

**Join the startup (O2), diligenced like an investor would.** Get cash-in-bank/runway numbers, not
just the pitch; confirm real technical mentorship given Alex's two years of experience; check the
equity grant against the ~0.6%-4.1% founding-engineer range (F11). Best if the founders are an
unusually strong match and Alex wants maximum ownership fastest, accepting the weakest base rates
and reversibility here. Unknown visa/location constraints bite hardest on this path. Wrong signal:
undifferentiated engineering work, or a next round that keeps slipping in the first 90 days.

## 6. Taste advice

Pick the next 12 months by what a stranger could verify at the end of them — a shipped feature
with outside users, a benchmark others cite, a grant decision, an offer letter — not by how
productive the year felt from the inside. Equally diligent paths diverge entirely in how legible
they are to the next hiring committee, admissions committee, or investor a year later. [inferred]

## 7. Where this analysis might be systematically biased

My inputs are whatever could be measured: median time-to-degree, median equity, seed-to-Series-A
rates, star/contributor counts. That overweights legible averages and underweights what the brief
cannot quantify — a PI's attentiveness, co-founders' chemistry, how Alex tolerates a quiet year.
Medians also describe modal outcomes, while O2 and O4's real payoffs run closer to power-law, so
this framing can undersell the tail scenarios that make those paths worth considering at all.
F13's Nobel-level framing of protein ML may also inflate the field's prestige relative to Alex's
smaller options. No stake removes motivated reasoning, but may make the safer-looking choice (O1)
seem better simply because its risks have the most citable numbers, not because they are smaller.

## 8. Where this judgment is most likely wrong

If Alex's risk tolerance is well above "moderate," O4 (and maybe O2) should rank above O1. If this
new PI is unusually well-funded or attentive — plausible, since new PIs vary and the brief has no
distinguishing data — O3's optionality and fit are understated. If science-team roles hard-gate on
a PhD more than my self-check suggests, O1's ceiling without one is lower than scored. F6 and F10,
load-bearing for O3's and O2's risk picture, rest on sources flagged as weakly reachable.
