**Claim (one line):** RLVR most likely did not teach entirely new reasoning patterns, but it is still a real capability lever: it makes the test-time-compute affordance of language priors reliable.

**Argument:** Yue et al. (2025-04, NeurIPS oral): RLVR mainly raises pass@1 and sampling efficiency; at large k the base model's pass@k can match the RL model; distillation introduces new reasoning patterns, RLVR does not. This supports the "resampling account", and I do not shy away from it. But that does not mean RLVR is unimportant: [synthesized, dossier §C] reasoning is a special kind of action, a bad trade in classical RL, but combined with language priors it lets an agent use compute flexibly at test time and generalize across tasks. RLVR's value is not in inventing new reasoning but in making a fragile affordance reliable and reproducible; the premise of ReAct (2022) is exactly this. Limitation: Yue et al. tested specific benchmarks, and it is not ruled out that RLVR induced different strategies on out-of-distribution tasks.

**My R1 scores on related directions:** D02 7

**What fact would change my judgment:** If an experiment shows that the post-RLVR model can solve out-of-distribution tasks, I withdraw "only resampling" and raise D02.
