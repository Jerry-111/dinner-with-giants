**Claim (one line):** RLVR mostly resamples and polishes existing capabilities; there is no evidence it can teach new reasoning patterns outside the training distribution.

**Argument:** Yue et al. (NeurIPS 2025 oral): RLVR raises pass@1 and sampling efficiency; with large enough k, the base model's pass@k can match or exceed the RL model; what can introduce new reasoning patterns is distillation, not RLVR (brief.md D02). Experience: when OpenAI built Universe, RL would first find the gap between the objective and the true intent (dossier §A2, E01/E30); in the Dwarkesh interview I said that RL optimized against an LLM judge produces gibberish that is nonetheless scored full marks (dossier §G3, E03) [quoted/synthesized]. Lesson: when the reward only reweights existing capability, what gets optimized is behavior that pleases the signal, not a new capability. What RLVR selects is exactly the paths the base model already knows and that are easier to verify. Where the analogy fails: if the reward comes from a multi-turn environment that keeps introducing new information, this limitation may not hold.

**What fact would change my judgment:** A preregistered controlled experiment showing that RLVR-trained models reliably solve out-of-distribution tasks the base model cannot get even with unlimited sampling; then I would raise D02.
