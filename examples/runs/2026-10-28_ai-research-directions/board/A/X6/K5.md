# X6 · A opening · Yann LeCun (K5)
> Stance from: Minority view that outlasted the deep-learning winter and won; now bets on world models in an abstract representation space
> Interests: AMI Labs executive chairman (2025-12-); bets on world models, not LLMs
> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words.

**Claim (one line):** Simulation/synthetic data is an auxiliary tool that feeds RL, not a road to understanding; signals such as contact and touch cannot be supplied by simulation.

**Argument:** Simulation can generate action sequences, but it cannot generate details that cannot be predicted (friction, deformation, contact force), and those are exactly the parts a machine most needs to understand. Experience argument: I worked on systems similar to world models for about fifteen years, and the first ten were mostly failures, because I kept training generative models that predict pixels/details [synthesized] (dossier§A5/§I, E09); only the shift to joint embeddings in 2020–21 found the right direction — the harder a generative method tries to cover unpredictable details, the more it fails; what should be learned is prediction in representation space, not generating details. The analogy may not hold: what failed back then was pixel-level video prediction, not simulation itself; SIMPLER (2024-05) shows that once the control and visual gaps are mitigated, simulated evaluation correlates strongly with real performance, which I accept; but for contact/tactile, where simulation engines model coarsely, the gap will not be solved by "mitigating the visual gap".

**My R1 scores on related directions:** L3 10, L4 35, D09 4, D06 8.

**What fact would change my judgment:** If policies trained purely in simulation, without ever seeing real contact data, can match real-data training in success rate on real contact tasks across multiple teams.
