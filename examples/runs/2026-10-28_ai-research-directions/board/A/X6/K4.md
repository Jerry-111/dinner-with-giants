**Claim (one line):** Simulation/synthetic data is a real lever, but contact/tactile is where it lies the most.

**Argument:** MimicGen amplifies about 200 real demonstrations into more than 50,000 across 18 tasks, a bar far lower than training a foundation model or accumulating a fleet; it is a data engine that people without a fleet can use. Experience: at Tesla, simulation was only one source of ground truth, not the only one; in an interview from the same period I said that in simulation an RL agent will exploit friction loopholes in the physics engine to slide (dossier §A4/§G1, E30) [quoted]. Lesson: a data engine can amplify a small sample, but a simulator lies most easily where modeling is weakest, such as contact force and friction (brief.md D06 cites Brooks: tactile force data is hard to supply through video demonstrations). Applied to X6: sim can substitute for visual diversity and long-tail coverage, but not for contact-rich tasks. Where the analogy fails: Tesla's data engine closes the loop by having the fleet keep feeding back real failure cases; pure simulation has no such loop.

**What fact would change my judgment:** Independent third-party results showing that the sim2real success rate on contact/tactile tasks can approach the level of vision/locomotion tasks; I would then lower the weight of this limitation.
