> Stance from: Bet on the same problem for nearly fifty years, changing institutions seven or eight times; now bets on continual learning and the experience paradigm
> Interests: Oak Lab co-founder, fundraising (2026-); bets on learning from experience, not on simulation
> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words.

**Claim (one line):** Simulation/synthetic data cannot substitute for scarce real data, especially in contact and long-tail scenarios; it should be a research tool, not a data factory.

**Argument:** When Keen built Physical Atari, a trained policy moved to a second robot assembled to the same blueprints got worse across all six games, and recovered only after continuing to learn on the new body [synthesized, dossier §I row 5]. Lesson: even "same-model" hardware is physically different; a policy has to keep correcting its own model after deployment and cannot be fixed by a single round of training. How it applies to this table's question: even between two nearly identical robots there are differences simulation cannot remove, so the gap between simulation and reality can only be wider — this is where the idea that any simulation is a tiny subset of the real world comes from [synthesized, dossier §C, E02/E13]. Where the analogy fails: this is a small sample from an early startup (two robots, six games), and engineering problems such as servo wear and current spikes could also be the cause; hardware immaturity cannot be ruled out, as opposed to the laws of physics themselves.

**My R1 scores on related directions:** D09 3, D06 8.

**What fact would change my judgment:** If, on contact-rich manipulation tasks with non-fixed geometry, the success rate of pure-simulation zero-shot deployment is replicated by a third party up to parity with real-data training, I would raise D09.
