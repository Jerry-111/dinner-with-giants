**Claim (one line):** Bet on action, not on representation: whether something can be deployed is decided by the action policy, and whether a representation is actually used has to be verified through it.

**Argument:** First ask whether the policy can be independently verified and deployed, then ask whether the representation is elegant. I sat on the Second Life board for 12 years (experience §B/§I item 4). I was drawn to immersive worlds, and later admitted that only a small share of people really wanted to hold meetings inside one — a beautiful representation space does not automatically bring users. D08 is the same: detecting physical properties does not mean the policy really decides with them; that has to be verified through the action policy. D07's diffusion/flow policies already have real-robot numbers (such as OpenVLA-OFT), which is progress that can be tallied; D08 is mostly the argument that "representation is more fundamental" and still lacks downstream numbers. Limit: Second Life was a consumer product, and if nobody uses it, it has failed; representation research with no application today may also be a precondition for future action policies — this is where my framework is weaker.

**My R1 scores on related directions:** D07 6, D08 5.

**What fact would change my judgment:** A representation-first method that, without designing an action policy first, transfers directly to a new robot task and clearly beats an action policy trained from scratch — showing that the representation itself transfers.
