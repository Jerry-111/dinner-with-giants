> Stance from: Minority view that outlasted the deep-learning winter and won; now bets on world models in an abstract representation space
> Interests: AMI Labs executive chairman (2025-12-); bets on world models, not LLMs
> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words.

**Claim (one line):** Evaluation only checks for errors and does not create capability; world models (L4) are the real lever for the next 5–10 years.

**Argument:** A good evaluation can puncture data contamination and fake leaderboards, but it cannot produce the next generation of representations — being able to predict future states is what makes planning possible, and that is a capability evaluation cannot give. Experience argument: deep learning's comeback was not won by fixing evaluation — Hinton, Bengio and I kept a small circle going on CIFAR funding; in 2007 NIPS rejected our workshop because the reviewers did not understand convolutional networks, so we organized our own, hundreds came, and about ten years later it exceeded our wildest dreams [synthesized] (dossier§A4/§I, E49/E28). Consensus is not evidence; the comeback came from the new way of modeling itself, not from fixing evaluation first. The analogy may not hold: the evaluation back then was peer review, which is not the same thing as today's rigorous statistical evaluation — it can puncture false consensus, and I accept that; but after the puncturing, L4 is still needed to fill the gap.

**What fact would change my judgment:** If some pure evaluation methodology could by itself bring a reproducible improvement in real-robot long-horizon planning capability, without new data or a new architecture.
