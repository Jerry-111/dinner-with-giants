**Claim (one line):** Evaluation is the underlying mechanism of trust: without it, none of the progress reported by data, simulation or world models can be told real from false.

**Argument:** Not being able to tell three things apart means having no evaluation: whether the progress is real, whether it is resampling an existing capability, or whether the system is grading itself. Before I worked as an analyst I first interviewed about 20 customers to understand that they wanted analysis that could change a judgment; later I distilled the "ten factors of revenue quality" — first ask whether the number is real and who it flows to (experience §A2/§I item 2). Applied to D08: I accept V-JEPA2's real-robot numbers, but "the physical quantity can be read out" and "the policy really uses it to decide" are two different things, and evaluation is what separates them. Limitation: financial fraud is usually deliberate, while evaluation bias (brief D05 "consistency-bias paradox") is more often a lack of methodological rigor than an intent to deceive — but the consequence is the same: you think you see progress, and it is really noise.

**My R1 scores on related directions:** D05 8, D08 5, D09 7, D06 6.

**What fact would change my judgment:** A narrow-domain world model, with no third-party evaluation as gatekeeper, is deployed directly by several independent teams and produces auditable cost-saving figures — this would show that evaluation is not a required gate.
