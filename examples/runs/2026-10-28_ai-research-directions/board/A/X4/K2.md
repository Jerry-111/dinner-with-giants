**Claim (one line):** Self-improvement is not a narrative packaged out of compute; it is a new capability that appears once loop speed crosses a threshold.

**Argument:** In 1999–2004 we moved the search index into memory; only after latency crossed an order-of-magnitude threshold could new features such as synonym expansion be built — not faster, but something new that could not be done before (dossier §A row 3, §I point 2). Lesson: when some quantity crosses an order-of-magnitude threshold, what it unlocks is a new capability, not an amplified version of the old one. The self-improvement loop works the same way: when the loop is fast enough to run thousands of iterations a day instead of the few dozen a person runs by hand, paths appear that researchers would not think of, and this applies in any domain with a measurable goal. The analogy may not hold: in the memory move, "latency" was a quantity that could be measured accurately; for self-improvement, as to "what was improved", the measurement itself may not be reliable (METR expenditure horizon; Sakana: about 42% of experiments failed because of code errors, brief D03) — if the measurement is inaccurate, "crossing the threshold" does not stand, and this is exactly the heart of what C4 and M1 question.

**My R1 scores on related directions:** D03 8, D04 6

**What fact would change my judgment:** If compute-normalized, pre-registered multi-round experiments show that the improvement comes mainly from repeated retries rather than a stronger method, I would lower D03.
