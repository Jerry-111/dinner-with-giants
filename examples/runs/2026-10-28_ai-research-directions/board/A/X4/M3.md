**Claim (one line):** "Self-improvement" is two things: lightweight, training-free experience accumulation is real but bounded; evidence for large-scale compounding RSI is thin, and it looks more like a narrative packaged out of compute spending.

**Argument:** "Self-improvement" gets lumped into one thing, but it is really two. First: my Agent Workflow Memory (about 22.5% improvement on WebArena, ICML 2025) shows that training-free, model-agnostic experience accumulation is real and reproducible [quoted, dossier §I]. Second: this accumulation is bounded — once past workflows have been distilled it tops out and does not snowball on its own; it is not the RSI that K2/M2 describe but one-time distillation. For large-scale self-improvement I have no first-hand evidence; it is closer to the METR/Sakana evidence that M1 cited. Experience argument: OpenHands gives the agent only five or six tools, betting that model capability will flatten the scaffolding, not hoping the scaffolding itself gets smarter [synthesized, dossier §I]. What it taught me: first ask whether the improvement concerns a problem the model could not solve, or is a temporary gain piled up by compute. Where the analogy may not hold: if RSI involves rewriting the training process itself, it may be a mechanism of a completely different order from scaffolding.

**My R1 scores on related directions:** D03 7, D04 5

**What fact would change my judgment:** Compute-normalized, pre-registered multi-round experiments showing that the capability gain from agent self-improvement clearly exceeds plain more-trial-and-error / more-sampling, and still holds after switching to a different task domain.
