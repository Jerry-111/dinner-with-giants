**Claim (one line):** In current self-improvement reports, most of the cost is compute trial and error rather than methodological progress; the evidence does not support betting heavily on it as a scarce compounding mechanism.

**Argument:** METR measured a strong NanoGPT baseline and found that in most trajectories 70-90% of the cost is compute spending, not methodological progress [technical evidence, brief.md]. This is the same kind of lesson as my own three years of FMTI tracking: reporting a number and that number reflecting a real trend are two different things — FMTI was published three years in a row, and the industry average fell from 58 to 40 [quoted, dossier§I, M1-V1-E08]. The self-improvement mechanisms that K2 and M3 describe currently have no compute-normalized, pre-registered controlled experiments; their evidence chain is much thinner than that for counterexamples such as Sakana AI Scientist's roughly 42% experiment failures. Limit of the analogy: FMTI is thin because nobody is willing to tell the truth, while RSI may be thin only because the direction is too new and has not yet been carefully measured; that does not mean the mechanism does not exist.
