# X2 · A opening · Jeff Dean (K2)
> Stance from: 27-year Google veteran: bet on scale and built TPU and Gemini; now resigned, betting on automating the experiment loop
> Interests: Discovery Loop CEO since 2026-08; Google invests and supplies compute
> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words.

**Claim (one line):** Once the team and compute are in place and a real need drives the work, it is real impact; waiting for a third-party evaluation to approve it is putting the cart before the horse.

**Argument:** MapReduce and Bigtable were extracted by our team from parallel code we kept writing by hand, and the papers were written in enough detail that outsiders could build open-source versions (dossier §A row 4, §I point 3). Lesson: good tools come from real, recurring needs; be the user of your own tool first; third-party evaluation was never a precondition. The same holds for an academic narrow-domain model: a complementary team of 2–4, compute usable continuously for months, plus a concrete task that really forces the training — that is real impact, and the evidence is whether anyone used it afterward, not whether it first cleared an evaluation gate. The analogy fails at one point: our "real need" came from Google's production load, which happens every day; an academic team often has to find that problem itself first, and this step is not covered by my experience — before a real problem is found, third-party evaluation is the only external check that can guard against "self-amusement"; C2 stands on this point.

**What fact would change my judgment:** If, among academic narrow-domain models, those backed by third-party evaluation show clearly higher external adoption than those with self-built evaluation, I would conclude the evaluation bar matters more.
