**Claim (one line):** Whether an academic lab training a narrow-domain foundation model counts as real impact: the bar is whether a credible third-party evaluation exists (C2), not whether team size and compute are sufficient (C3/C4).

**Argument:** In R1 I selected C1, C2 and C5, and did not select C3 or C4. Web-SSL (2025-04, co-authored with LeCun): a controlled comparison showed that pure-vision self-supervised learning scaled to 7B is still not saturated and reaches CLIP-comparable level — a product of controlled design, not of frontier compute [synthesized, E10]. Experience argument: my most enduring works, DiT and V*, were both two-person teams with compute far below frontier labs, but the controls were clean and outsiders could verify and adopt them at once (V* was cited by OpenAI) — this taught me that impact depends on whether it can be independently verified, not on how many GPUs are burned (dossier §I rows 1 and 3). The bottleneck for an academic lab is whether it can design reproducible controlled experiments: without compute you are only slower; without evaluation, no one believes what you did. Where it may not hold: a compute threshold for long-horizon simulation such as D08 may be real — it took AMI Labs $1 billion to be able to build H-JEPA; once the scale is large enough, compute is a hard constraint.
