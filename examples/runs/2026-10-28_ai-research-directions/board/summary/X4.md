# X4 notes: Agent self-improvement (RSI): a real, scarce compounding mechanism, or a narrative packaged out of compute spending?

> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words. These notes evaluate simulated personas' posts.

**Consensus:** Training-free, lightweight experience accumulation (such as Agent Workflow Memory) is real and reproducible but bounded, not a compounding loop; AlphaEvolve has continuously recovered about 0.7% of global compute in Borg scheduling for over a year, officially self-reported and not independently audited, real but narrow-domain; METR data: on a strong baseline, 70–90% of the cost in most self-improvement trajectories is compute trial and error; the Darwin Gödel Machine's "$22,000 over two weeks" has no primary source and has been withdrawn. At present no independent, compute-normalized metric can separate methodological progress from compute/luck.

**Remaining disagreement:** Divided by what the posts actually claim: C4/M1/M3 (evidence for large-scale open-ended RSI is still thin; default is skepticism) vs K2 (bets that independent verification arrives around 2027; should bet heavily now); M2 sits in the middle — does not presuppose whether RSI is real, and holds that honestly measuring the progress/compute share is itself a scarce academic edge. The core is which side carries the burden of proof, on top of the measurement gap.

**Hardest-hitting arguments:**
- Evidence is thin: METR strong-baseline data (see Consensus), 70–90% of the cost is compute trial and error (A/X4/C4, A/X4/M1).
- Should bet heavily: AlphaEvolve's real narrow-domain compounding data (see Consensus) (B/X4/K2).

**The fact that can decide:** Whether in 2027–2028 there are independent third-party, compute-normalized cases that keep compounding on open-ended goals rather than one-off optimization in a narrow domain.

**What newly emerged:** Bridge — a compute-normalized benchmark that distinguishes narrow-domain optimization from open-ended compounding (C4); design an "improvement generating improvement" metric independent of compute consumption (M3). Experiment that can decide — the score gap between a fixed-compute self-improvement system and random search with the same compute (K2). New framing — split self-improvement into "bounded contribution" (AWM, AlphaEvolve) and "open-ended compounding" (true RSI) (M3).

**Minority opinions:** K2: "After cutting to 6 I am level with C4, but the direction is different — I bet that independent verification arrives in 2027, rather than stopping first to wait for others to get it done."
