# X1 notes: Is evaluation (L1) the top lever, or are data/world models more critical?

> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words. These notes evaluate simulated personas' posts.

**Consensus:** Evaluation does not create capability/new representations; it only checks for errors, recognizes progress as real, and tells where a capability comes from; peer review ≠ statistical evaluation, and the CNN comeback was recognized sooner thanks to the ImageNet evaluation; open-world robots lack the "clean" self-generated right/wrong signals that games/code/math have.

**Remaining disagreement:** Not a factual disagreement but one of weight/boundary: whether evaluation should rank ahead of output (world models/data/new architectures); K3 assigns automatically verifiable evaluation to L2 and does not recognize the rest.

**Hardest-hitting arguments:**
Evaluation-first side — M7: real-robot trials cannot be repeated, so statistical design should matter as much as the model (A/X1/M7); C2: an unstable judge (GPT-4o-mini changes its answer 13.6% of the time on repeated judgments) is exactly the reason to bet on evaluation (B/X1/C2).
Data/world-model-first side — K5: the comeback came from the new way of modeling itself, not from fixing evaluation first (A/X1/K5); K3: benchmarks/judges only say whether the score is high, not whether the model knows it is right or wrong (A/X1/K3).

**The fact that can decide:** In 2027–2028, if a world model/representation is shown in independent teams' real-robot long-horizon tasks to "encode the property" and to be something "the policy really relies on to decide" — the data/world-model side holds; if this cannot be done, or the model leads only inside the training distribution — the evaluation side holds.

**What newly emerged:** A task-agnostic, third-party-executable causal separation protocol for "representation encoding vs policy use"; under fixed compute, comparing the long-term deployment performance of "self-proof-driven" and "evaluation-driven" iteration; designing a sufficiently hard self-generated terminal signal for open-world robots.

**Minority opinions (excerpt):**
K3: "I disagree with conflating recognizing with producing: AlexNet's capability came from convolutional networks, not from ImageNet ... it should add points, not override L2/L4/L6."
K5: "Evaluation's academic edge is real but limited in size; world models/data have a higher potential ceiling; that is why I still give L4 the higher score."

**Overtime (Yann LeCun vs. Russ Tedrake):** Converges on causal order — whether the capability appears first and evaluation confirms it afterward, or evaluation comes first; the Bell Labs pattern is hard to apply to robots, which lack an equally hard terminal signal. The disagreement is one of values/time scale: Tedrake treats researchers as portfolio managers, LeCun treats them as individuals with a single bet. What can decide: in 2027–28, whether the new architecture succeeds first and evaluation is added afterward, or whether, without evaluation design, success cannot be recognized.
