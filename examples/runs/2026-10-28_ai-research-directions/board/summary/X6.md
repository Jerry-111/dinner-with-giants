# X6 notes: Can simulation and synthetic data substitute for scarce real robot data, especially for contact, touch and the long tail?

> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words. These notes evaluate simulated personas' posts.

**Consensus:** Domain randomization combines new signals within the prior, a real gain for visual diversity/long tail rather than pure reuse; MimicGen amplifies about 200 demonstrations into 50,000+, a bar far lower than training a foundation model (all sides accept). Simulation has a weak spot on contact/tactile scenes: on Physical Atari, changing the robot body degraded all six games, and unmodeled physical differences show through.

**Remaining disagreement:** Not whether it is useful, but whether "most" counts as simulation's credit and whether amplification counts as "new information" — an empirical disagreement, not one of values, waiting for real-robot replication in 2027–28.

**Hardest-hitting arguments:**
Pro side: Only after a layer of real touch is mixed in can a world model's representation drive contact decisions (B/X6/C3); the real value of seed amplification for the long tail should not be brushed aside (C/X6/K4).
Con side: Real-robot grasping experience: new information must be injected through real interaction (A/X6/C5); not only contact, but any unmodeled physical difference shows through (B/X6/K3); in fifteen years of world-model work, pixel prediction mostly failed (A/X6/K5).

**The fact that can decide:** On contact-rich tasks, whether pure-simulation policies that do not rely on real data can be replicated by a third party and match the real baseline; the answer will be clear in 2027–28.

**What newly emerged:** Treat the degree of contact-richness as a continuous variable to find the threshold at which simulation's gain goes to zero; run a controlled comparison of the domain-randomization / real-data mixing ratio to find the inflection point of the replacement curve; score D09 in two segments, real-feedback closed loop vs pure simulation.

**Minority opinions:**
C3: "The evidence points to one slice, contact/tactile; on visual diversity, the long tail and low-dimensional dynamics, simulation is still a real lever."
C5 (excerpt): "It is a real lever only within the subset where the physical prior is modeled correctly, and contact/tactile still needs real data as the base."
K3 (excerpt): "What I still disagree with is whether 'most' should count as simulation's credit."
K4: "The real value of seed amplification on dimensions other than fine-grained physics should not be brushed aside with 'does not count as new information'."

**Overtime (Sergey Levine vs. Andrej Karpathy):** The inflection point is a continuous function of contact-force calibration precision, not a fixed ceiling; amplification does not create new facts outside the prior but does create real training signal (QT-Opt evidence). Moved on to a more direct test: 200 real seeds alone vs adding 50,000 simulation amplifications, the gap in held-out long-tail success rate. The two characterize the disagreement differently: Levine says time scale, Karpathy says an empirical question.
