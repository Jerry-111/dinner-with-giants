# X7 notes: Did RLVR teach new reasoning capabilities, or is it polishing/resampling existing capabilities?

> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words. These notes evaluate simulated personas' posts.

**Consensus:** Three core facts, Yue et al. (NeurIPS 2025 Oral), s1 and DeepSeek-R1 (confirmed in errata_B): RLVR mainly raises pass@1/sampling efficiency; at large k the base model's pass@k can match or exceed the RL model; new reasoning patterns come from distilling a stronger teacher. Being distillable ≠ having taught new out-of-distribution patterns — several people independently pointed this out in round C, and the side that originally held "taught new capability" (C3, C4) moved to the "affordance becomes reliable" framing to describe RLVR's value.

**Remaining disagreement:** The factual disagreement on "taught / did not teach new out-of-distribution patterns" has converged; what remains is a value judgment — how many points "reliable execution" deserves: C3/C4 hold it is a decisive lever and deserves a mid-high score; K4/K6/M2/M6 lean toward counting it as an efficiency score, not a new-paradigm score. This is a disagreement of values, not of facts.

**Hardest-hitting arguments:**
- Resampling side: In Yue et al., under strictly verifiable rewards, at large k the base model's pass@k matches or exceeds the RL model (A/X7/K4).
- Side that says it deserves weight: s1 raised AIME24 from 50.0 to 56.7 with only 7 H100-GPU-hours; a gain this large at small compute is hard to explain by pure resampling (A/X7/C3).

**The fact that can decide:** Whether an RLVR model of the same size with no stronger teacher, after self-distillation, can reliably exceed the large-k ceiling on out-of-distribution tasks that the base model cannot get with large-k sampling (within 2027–28).

**What newly emerged:** Compare RLVR-distilled output against the base model's large-k sampling plus filtering at the same compute, on out-of-distribution tasks — several people independently proposed the same experiment, and it has converged into a shared test design. New framing: "reliability lever" and "new-structure lever" are two independent dimensions, not the taught / did not teach binary.

**Minority opinions:**
- C3: "Not fully applicable — on mechanism I have moved to the majority position; I still disagree on one point: how many points the reliability mechanism deserves."
- C4 (excerpt): "D02 stays at mid-high, because reliable deployment value is underrated."

**Overtime (Jensen Huang vs. Andrej Karpathy):** Mechanism disagreement cleared to zero: under strictly verifiable rewards, large k still catches up with the RL model; the AlexNet analogy does not hold — a narrow claim does not amount to setting a wide ceiling. The disagreement is one of values: Huang holds that reliable execution should be scored by industry scale; Karpathy holds that, judged by whether it is new, it counts as an efficiency score. What can decide: whether the drop in inference cost comes mainly from reliability investment or from pre-training scale.
