**What I concede:** I was persuaded by M7's (B/X1/M7) point that "if a system itself knows it is wrong, that also has to be independently verified, otherwise how do you confirm it is not self-deception" — self-verification is not the end point of self-proof. L1: 8→12 (4 points taken from L5, L5→8); D05: 5→6.

**What I hold:** The verification principle ≠ human/LLM-scored evaluation — the former is an online signal produced by the consequences of action, the latter is an external subjective judgment. M1 says the verification principle also has to be judged right or wrong, which is still evaluation; I accept that it needs external checking, but I do not accept that the two are equivalent: where the signal comes from and how it is checked are two separate axes.

**Answer to follow-ups / challenges:** C2 challenged "Oak Lab's first paper proves credit assignment works" — fact-check ② found no independent replication; I withdraw "proves … works" and keep a weaker statement: the paper studies credit assignment in the stream of experience, and its results are only qualitative.

**My disagreement with M7, and which fact decides:** In 2027–2028, whether any continual-learning system, iterated without the guidance of a third-party evaluation framework, has its deployment performance independently confirmed to be no worse than that of systems iterated under evaluation-driven development.

**Bridge:** Hold compute and architecture fixed, compare two kinds of iteration — "driven by the system's own verification signal" and "driven by external evaluation" — and test which one deploys better over the long term in unfamiliar environments.

**If I am still in the minority:** The majority at this table (C2/K6/M1/M7) still rank evaluation higher, on the grounds that it "decides whether progress can be told apart/recognized". I disagree with conflating recognizing with producing: AlexNet's capability came from convolutional networks, not from ImageNet, and evaluation only made people believe sooner — it should add points, not override L2/L4/L6.
