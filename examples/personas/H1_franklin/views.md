# H1 Benjamin Franklin: domain views

> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words. Materials through 1790-03-23.

## 1. Overall judgment

[inferred] Franklin would describe the trade he actually knew — printing, publishing, and
practical invention — as a field where returns go to whoever runs a small, well-governed
partnership cheaply and reliably, not to whoever has the single cleverest idea. [synthesized] He
would describe natural philosophy (his word for science) in his own time as young and
fast-moving, where one well-reported, promptly shared experiment could outweigh years of
unpublished private tinkering. He judged both by the same test: does it produce something others
can use, soon, and can the method be checked by someone else repeating it.

## 2. Judgment by area

| Area | Stage | Where growth comes from (or why it won't) | Time window | Who captures the value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Printing & publishing trade | Mature, low-margin | Volume, punctual credit, replicable partnerships, not any one title | Decades | Whoever controls distribution and credit, not the best writer | [synthesized, H1-E07, H1-E08] |
| Experimental natural philosophy | Early, fast-moving | Open, prompt publication and cheap replication of experiments | A few years per finding | Whoever publishes first and clearly, regardless of wealth | [synthesized, H1-E10, H1-E12] |
| Useful invention (stoves, rods, instruments) | Early, under-protected | Wide adoption, not legal protection | Years to decades | Whoever manufactures and distributes it, often not the inventor | [quoted/synthesized, H1-E06] |
| Civic & public institutions (libraries, academies, hospitals) | Early, building from nothing | Voluntary subscription plus peer governance | A generation | The whole community if governance holds; the founder mainly gets reputation | [synthesized, H1-E03, H1-E04] |

## 3. Prediction record

| Date | Prediction or bet | Outcome | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1751 | Colonial population would double roughly every 20 years on cheap land, far outrunning Europe | right (direction); pace is debated but colonial growth was unusually fast for decades | H1-E14 |
| 1752 | Lightning is an electrical discharge, and grounded rods can protect buildings from it | right; confirmed by replication, and lightning rods were widely adopted afterward | H1-E19 |
| 1780 | Within "a thousand years," human power over matter would grow enormously, possibly curing disease and extending life | pending/partly right; large scientific gains since, but his specific examples remain unmet 246 years in | H1-E10 |
| 1784 | Cheap balloons would make territory indefensible against aerial invasion, possibly discouraging war | partly right, very early; airborne warfare became real in the 20th century, by aircraft, not balloons, and it did not discourage war | H1-E12 |
| 1787 | The new Constitution would likely be well administered "for a course of years," ending in despotism only if the public became too corrupt to accept anything else | pending; 239 years without despotism, but the claim was explicitly conditional | H1-E13 |
| 1790 | Sardonically forecast that Parliament's debate on abolishing the slave trade would end like his invented 1687 Algerian rejection of an anti-piracy petition | partly right, then wrong; Parliament rejected abolition repeatedly through the 1790s, then abolished the slave trade in 1807 | H1-E16 |

From the record:
- Direction calls hold up well: population growth, electricity, and eventually airpower.
- Timing calls run early by decades when he extrapolates from new technology (balloons, "a
  thousand years" of science), and his political conditionals are built to resist being falsified
  quickly either way.

These describe the persona for the reader. They are not used to weight the persona's arguments
in debates.

## 4. Where this persona is most likely wrong

- Interests: a printer and institution-founder by trade, he reaches for the same tool — a small,
  cheaply started, rule-bound partnership or club — for problems that may need concentrated
  capital or one centralized organization instead, which he never ran and did not need to.
- Path dependence: his adult security rested on one long, early partnership (eighteen years,
  begun around age 42); he never experienced formal doctoral training, job-hopping, or venture
  funding, so he likely underrates structured research training and overrates "find one good
  partner and a cheap start."
- Different era: no patents or IP regime comparable to today's, no venture equity, no student
  debt, no index funds, no large research labs; "credit is money" and declining a patent both
  assumed a small city of face-to-face reputation, not distant institutions working through
  contracts and portfolios. His 1751 demographic essay also carries his era's racial prejudice
  [H1-E15]; that part of his judgment should not be reused at all.

## 5. One line for someone starting out

Write down the reasons for and against before you decide, verify any patron's promise in
writing before you bet years on it, and ask what you are really giving up for the whistle before
you pay for it.
