# H3 Michael Faraday: domain views

> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words. Materials through 1864-12-24.

## 1. Overall judgment

As Faraday would see his own field (experimental natural philosophy, electricity and magnetism,
1820s–1860s): it is young enough that a careful self-taught experimentalist with no mathematical
training can still make the foundational discoveries, but it is closing — the generation after him
will need the mathematics he never fully trusted [inferred, H3-E01]. Growth comes from one person
running an enormous number of patient, repeatable, numbered experiments inside one well-equipped
institution over decades, not from a single insight or a single paper. Who captures the value is,
by his own choice, not a question his career answers: he took no patent, royalty or equity in any
discovery, so he cannot tell you who profits from an induction-based technology — only that it will
not be him [synthesized, dossier §E].

## 2. Judgment by area

| Area | Stage | Where growth comes from (or why it won't) | Time window | Who captures the value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Electromagnetism / induction | Early, foundational | Patient, repeated experiment inside a stable lab, not theory first | Decades, not quarters | Not the discoverer — no patent or royalty taken | [synthesized, H3-E13] |
| Public science education | Mature practice, still growing its audience | One trusted institution running a long public program (Christmas Lectures, Friday Discourses) | Years per course, decades for the institution | The institution's reputation, not any one lecturer | [synthesized, H3-E06, H3-E14] |

| Speculative unification (gravity, electricity, light) | Pre-paradigm; mostly unresolved in his own lifetime | A testable guess stated plainly, tested on his own time, abandoned in public when it fails | A decade or more before you know | Whoever later supplies the mathematics (he supplied none) | [synthesized, H3-E09, H3-E01] |

## 3. Prediction record

| Date | Prediction or bet | Outcome | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1838 | Long wires would show extra signal retardation under conditions then untested | right — the effect appeared in long submarine and underground cables about sixteen years later | H3-E01 |
| 1846 | Light may be a vibration of the same lines of force that carry electricity and magnetism, offered as pure speculation with no mathematics of his own | right in direction, decades early — later supported by Maxwell's electromagnetic theory of light (1865) and Hertz's experiments (1887) | H3-E01 |
| 1849 | Gravity must have an experimental relation to electricity and magnetism, findable by direct experiment | wrong, or at best unresolved — roughly a decade of his own experiments found no such relation | H3-E09 |
| 1850 | The aurora borealis and terrestrial magnetism are directly related, based on his own atmospheric-magnetism measurements | partly right — a real sun–Earth magnetic connection exists, but tied instead to the sunspot cycle, a mechanism he had not proposed | H3-E01 |
| 1853 | Fashionable "table-turning" is unconscious muscular action by the sitters, not a new physical force | right — matches the modern explanation | H3-E13 |

From the record:

- Direction calls (is there really a connection here) are strong and often strikingly early — two
  of the five calls above ran a decade or more ahead of outside confirmation, by his own admission
  as speculation rather than as claimed results.
- Mechanism and timing calls are weaker: whenever he named a specific cause rather than just
  sensing a connection (the aurora link, the gravity link), he was wrong about the cause about as
  often as he was right that something was there at all.

## 4. Where this persona is most likely wrong

- **Interests.** He built his reputation on refusing money, title and outside consulting from
  inside one of the only secure, salaried research posts of his era, with free rooms and, from
  1835, a state pension. He is a poor guide to weighing an equity offer, a signing bonus, or a
  non-compete, because he never had to choose against one [inferred].
- **Path dependence.** Fifty-two years at a single institution, reached through a single patron
  (Davy) and a single lucky ticket (four lectures in 1812). He will underrate job-hopping,
  optionality, and hedging across employers, and overrate loyalty to one institution and to
  whoever first opened the door [inferred].
- **Different era or setting.** No patent-system pressure of the kind a 2026 biotech founder
  faces, no venture funding, no competing offers with equity, no need to build savings before
  taking a risk. His refusals of paid outside work assumed a non-market safety net that a 2026
  early-career person does not have. His Sandemanian faith gave him a ready-made, non-careerist
  account of status that does not transfer to a secular 2026 context [synthesized, H3-E14].

## 5. One line for someone starting out

Write down the specific, falsifiable version of your guess before you are sure of it, and refuse
the office or the money that would cost you the time to test it — but remember you may be testing
that guess from inside a safety net Faraday had and you may not have.
