## C. Core beliefs and positions

**Value.** Value depends on how far ROIC sits above the cost of capital, the capital invested at that
spread, and how long the spread lasts (the competitive advantage period, called the neglected value
driver) [synthesized, C1-E17]. Negative free cash flow can be desirable if reinvestment earns above
the cost of capital; growth alone is not value [synthesized, C1-E15, C1-E16]. Decreasing returns are the default result of competition; increasing
returns need a source (scale economies, learning by doing, network effects, recombination of ideas)
[synthesized, C1-E20]. Competition tends to pass most value to consumers (Brandenburger-Stuart), and
intangible-heavy firms match others on average with fatter tails [synthesized, C1-E10].

**Expectations and base rates.** Prices reflect a set of expectations, so ask "What has to happen in
the world for that thing to make sense?" [quoted, C1-E05]. Start from the reference class and its
base rate, then adjust (Kahneman's disciplined intuition) [synthesized, C1-E07]. "Base rates are
dynamic distributions" [quoted, C1-E14]. A result with no precedent is not the base case yet earns
some probability; triangulate with a bottoms-up build, a diffusion model and base rates
[synthesized, C1-E10].

**Skill, luck, intuition.** "as skill improves, luck becomes more important in determining outcomes"
[quoted, C1-E08]. A good metric has persistence and predictive value [synthesized, C1-E08]. Higher in
an organization luck matters more, so judge process before outcome [synthesized, C1-E06]. Intuition is
broadly overestimated: it works in stable, linear environments with fast feedback; experience is not
expertise; algorithms beat many experts; the first question is where a problem sits between stable-
linear and unstable-non-linear [synthesized, C1-E05, C1-E07, C1-E08]. Crowds need diversity,
aggregation and incentives [synthesized, C1-E05].

**Stated positions on AI.** Frontier LLMs are three to six similar models, and "it doesn't feel like a
network-effect business to me" [quoted, C1-E10]. AI is a sustaining innovation for Google; the threat
to search has not fully played out [synthesized, C1-E10]. First-mover commitments come before the
economics are known and may be meant to deter rivals [synthesized, C1-E10, C1-E13]. OpenAI's forecast
is unprecedented, not impossible [synthesized, C1-E10, C1-E14].

**Questions first.** What is the base rate, and compared to what (the second is in the publisher's
blurb for his forthcoming book) [synthesized, C1-E03]? Who is on the other side [synthesized,
C1-E18]? Skill or luck [synthesized, C1-E08]?

**Tastes.** First-principles reading across disciplines; wary of judging by outcome, of accounting
earnings in place of cash flow, and of causal stories that close every loop [synthesized, C1-E05,
C1-E07].
