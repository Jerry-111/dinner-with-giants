# C1 Michael Mauboussin: domain views

> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words. Materials through 2026-09-22.

## 1. Overall judgment

As his 2026 notes and interviews frame it, the AI moment is a capital-spending boom: several of the
largest technology companies are investing heavily enough that their free cash flow is falling.
Adoption is fast: OpenAI's 2025 revenue was about $13 billion, up 250%, well ahead of expectations
[synthesized, C1-E10]. Spending is outrunning profit estimates: for five hyperscalers combined, free
cash flow fell from about $170 billion (2024Q1) to about $35 billion (2026Q2), with a
consensus trough near -$265 billion in 2027Q3, while return on incremental capital is forecast to fall
from a peak near 40% to about 23%, against a cost of capital near 8% [synthesized, C1-E15, C1-E16].
No bubble label appears in the materials read; he tracks whether incremental returns stay above the
cost of capital and flags that profit estimates have lagged capex estimates at most of the five
[synthesized, C1-E15, C1-E16]. Two forces drive it: diffusion speed, and preemption, meaning large
commitments made before the economics are known and possibly meant to deter rivals [synthesized,
C1-E10, C1-E13].

## 4. Where this persona is most likely wrong

- **Interests** (disclosed facts, dossier §E). As of 2026-11 he works at Morgan Stanley Investment
  Management's Counterpoint Global, whose team argues that companies applying AI, more than its
  suppliers, often create the most enduring value [synthesized, C1-E19]. Framework check: his view
  that competition passes value to consumers fails if a model provider keeps share and pricing power
  [inferred].
- **Path dependence.** He began as a packaged-food analyst, and his reference classes are listed U.S.
  companies since 1950 [synthesized, C1-E10]. Leading AI labs are private (he refers to private up
  rounds and possible listings) and the technology moves in weeks to months, so a listed-company
  class may lag [inferred]; the 2026-05 follow-up concedes that base rates move [synthesized, C1-E14].
- **Different era or setting.** His advice comes from large financial institutions and near-efficient
  markets. Early-career research is a less settled talent market, with organizations that reorganize
  quickly and one career path rather than many bets [inferred].

## 5. One line for someone starting out

Before choosing a topic or an organization, find the base rate for people who made the same bet, ask
who captures the value and for how long, and prefer small, reversible experiments with fast, accurate
feedback [synthesized, C1-E05, C1-E07, C1-E10].
