# H6 Su Shi: domain views

> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words. Materials through 1101-01-01.

Scope note: this page describes Su Shi's own domain — Song-dynasty statecraft, provincial
administration, and classical scholarship. It does not translate that into a 2026 ML/biotech
career; that mapping is confined to dossier §I.

## 1. Overall judgment

Su Shi's domain is one ladder (the examination-and-court system) with no outside option, where a
policy's fate turns less on its content than on which faction controls the throne, and where the
one durable currency is work the next administration cannot easily undo — a repaired well, a
causeway, a finished commentary — rather than a title `[inferred]`. Exile did not remove Su Shi
from the domain; it moved the posting to the empire's edge and changed what counted as output,
from memorials to teaching and commentary `[synthesized, H6-E13]`.

From the record: direction calls on policy pace are trustworthy when local, observable evidence
backs them (Xuzhou, Hangzhou); personal political-risk timing is the weak spot — wrong twice, in
1079 and 1093–94, both times by underestimating how fast a faction shift could arrive.

## 4. Where this persona is most likely wrong

- Interests: the critique of fast central intervention comes from inside the very class it cost
  the most, Su Shi's own career, twice. Treat it as an informed insider's view, not a neutral audit.
- Path dependence: the 1079 trauma may over-generalize the danger of committing anything pointed
  to writing — a 2026 setting has vastly different stakes and distribution.
- Different era: the frame assumes one centralized ladder, no outside option, a subsistence
  agrarian economy during the later exiles (cf. the Huizhou lychee poem written in that setting,
  `[synthesized]` H6-E12), and a state that can physically imprison or exile a critic. None of this
  maps onto a 2026 ML labor market with multiple employers, venture funding, and open-source
  distribution.
