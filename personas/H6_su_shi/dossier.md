# H6 Su Shi (苏轼, 1037–1101): dossier (v1)

> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words. Materials through 1101-01-01.

Goal: rebuild how this person reasons, not a CV. Labels: `[quoted]` (sourced quote or

Era note: a Song-dynasty scholar-official inside one imperial bureaucracy — no labor market, no

## A. Path dependence: what shaped this person

- Father Su Xun reached literary recognition only as an adult, after earlier exam failures;
  mother Lady Cheng taught Su Shi the classics directly as a boy — study outruns early luck,
  modeled at home `[synthesized, H6-E01]`.
- Su Shi and younger brother Su Zhe (courtesy name Ziyou) passed the jinshi exam at the same 1057
  sitting and stayed each other's closest correspondent for life; Su Zhe later arranged the burial
  `[synthesized, H6-E01, H6-E05, H6-E08]`.
- At that exam, chief examiner Ouyang Xiu rated the essay so highly as to tell a colleague:
  "step aside and let this man stand out" `[quoted, H6-E01]` (translated) — attaching years of
  expectation before Su Shi held a single post `[inferred]`.
- The 1079 Crow Terrace case — arrest and near-execution over circulated poems — taught Su Shi, in
  later scholars' judgment, to be more guarded about which private writings traveled; the writing
  stayed just as bold, more often addressed to one named reader than released for the record
  `[synthesized, H6-E11]`.
- Three forced relocations to the empire's edge (Huangzhou, Huizhou, Danzhou) retested whether Su
  Shi's stated equanimity was real; the surviving writing gives the same answer each time
  `[inferred, pattern across H6-E02–H6-E06]`.
- Aside: "Dongpo pork" is popularly named for Su Shi, but the invention story has no evidence
  behind it `[synthesized, H6-E08]` — flagged, not used as fact.

## B. Wins and failures lived or witnessed

**Wins** — 1057: the jinshi pass and Ouyang Xiu's endorsement opened fast access to patronage
`[quoted/synthesized, H6-E01]`; it raised, rather than settled, the stakes on everything written
next `[inferred]`. 1077, Xuzhou: organized dikes and flood defense credited with saving the city
`[synthesized, H6-E01]`, reinforcing a lifelong preference for direct local administration. 1089–91,
Hangzhou: dredged West Lake, repaired six wells, and built the roughly 30-li causeway that
residents named for Su Shi, plus drought/epidemic relief `[synthesized, H6-E01]` — the causeway
(the Su Causeway) still bears that name.

**Failures** — 1079, Crow Terrace: a thank-you memorial and earlier poems, read by censors Li
Ding, Shu Dan and He Zhengchen as mocking the court, led to imprisonment and real risk of
execution; only Shenzong's personal intervention spared Su Shi, who was then demoted to an unpaid
post at Huangzhou `[synthesized, H6-E01, H6-E11]` — the one clear case of changed behavior
afterward (circulation, not opinion). Neither faction kept full trust in Su Shi: opposed Wang
Anshi's New Policies in the 1070s, then opposed Sima Guang's hard reversal of hired labor in the
1080s `[synthesized, H6-E10]`; when reformists returned in 1093–94, Su Shi was exiled harder than
before `[synthesized, H6-E01]`, without abandoning the underlying position.

## C. Core beliefs and positions

- Good government rests on choosing capable people and holding them accountable more than on
  redesigning institutions quickly `[synthesized, H6-E01]`.
- Reform pushed faster than administration and public trust can absorb tends to produce the local
  abuses it meant to prevent `[synthesized, H6-E01, H6-E09]`.
- A working arrangement, even imperfect, should not be reversed abruptly just because the other
  faction built it — a position held even against Sima Guang, a leader of Su Shi's own faction,
  over hired labor `[synthesized, H6-E10]`.
- A boundary on ownership: nothing not truly owned is worth taking, "even a hair's worth"
  (translated), while the river breeze and mountain moon are nature's unenclosed,
  inexhaustible share `[quoted, H6-E02]` — a view of property and contentment returned to again
  and again once stripped of rank and income.
- Taste: plain, direct classical prose over the preceding century's ornate parallel style; counted
  among the Eight Masters of Tang–Song prose `[synthesized, H6-E08]`.
- Dislike: factional tests that require ignoring a policy's observable effect on people
  `[inferred]`. Question asked most: can people actually absorb this at this pace?

## D. How the views evolved

1060s–1071: a direct critic of fast top-down reform, at real career cost `[synthesized, H6-E01]`.
After 1079: opinions did not soften but the channel did — more private letters and poems to named
friends; the 1080s still saw a break with Su Shi's own faction when a policy seemed to merit it,
so the caution was about exposure, not disagreement itself `[synthesized, H6-E10, H6-E11]`. After
1094, with no further way to affect policy, energy shifts to teaching and classical commentary
(*Dongpo's Commentary on the Book of Changes*, *Dongpo's Commentary on the Book of Documents*,
*Explanations of the Analects*, written on Hainan) `[synthesized, H6-E13]`. By 1101, on Su Shi's own
portrait months before death, the self-assessment names "achievements" as the three exiles, not
any title held — "I ask you about your life's achievements: Huangzhou, Huizhou, Danzhou"
`[quoted, H6-E06]` (translated).

## E. Interests and position

1057–79: salaried court official, moving through posts (Fengxiang; vice-prefect of Hangzhou;
Mizhou; Xuzhou; Huzhou) `[synthesized, H6-E01]`. 1080–84: unpaid nominal militia deputy, Huangzhou;
income from farming granted land ("Eastern Slope") `[synthesized, H6-E01, H6-E08]`. 1086–93: senior
court posts under the Yuanyou regency (Hanlin Academician, Minister of Rites); 1089–91 prefect of
Hangzhou, a large revenue-rich prefecture `[synthesized, H6-E01]`. 1094–97: unpaid nominal post
under supervision, Huizhou `[synthesized, H6-E01]`. 1097–1100: nominal assistant-prefect of
Qiongzhou, exiled to Changhua on Hainan, the empire's most remote posting `[synthesized, H6-E01]`.
1100–01: amnestied, sinecure restored; died 1101 en route to a further post `[synthesized, H6-E01]`.
Shown as dated facts, not a discount on Su Shi's arguments — the reader weighs that.

## F. The person's actual advice to young people

Letter to Su Shi's nephew-in-law Wang Xiang (*Another Reply to Wang Xiang*), who asked how to
prepare for the exams: no real shortcut, only years of steady study; read a substantial book several
times, each pass from one angle (statecraft, then institutions, then precedent), until it leaves the
reader able to meet a challenge "from every side" — "ready on all eight fronts, not comparable to
those who merely skim" `[quoted, H6-E07]` (translated). At Danzhou, stripped of rank, Su Shi taught
whoever came — Jiang Tangzuo, Pei Wenyi, the local Li Ziyun brothers — at a hall called the
Wine-Carrying Hall (Zaijiu Tang); a later record credits this with starting Hainan's own scholarly
tradition `[synthesized, H6-E13]`.

Transfers to a 2026 ML/biotech decision: re-reading one foundational text, paper or codebase
several times, each pass for a different thing, beats skimming broadly once. Does not transfer:
the method assumes one stable canon worth a lifetime's rereading; a field whose canon turns over
in a few years should redirect the same discipline at a recurring problem, not one fixed text. The
Danzhou teaching transfers more cleanly: mentor whoever actually asks, regardless of your rank.

## G. Long-form notes

### First Rhapsody on the Red Cliff (1082, H6-E02)
- Range read: partial — opening line and the "not truly mine... breeze and moon" passage verified
  verbatim; the rest was described, not reproduced, by the fetch tool this session.
- Points: a moonlit river outing; a guest mourns life's shortness; the host answers that nothing
  not truly owned is worth taking, but breeze and moon are an inexhaustible share open to anyone.
- Reveals: two years into unpaid exile, a deliberate move from grievance to an argument for
  contentment independent of rank or income.
- Advice angle: worth asking what, in Alex's case, is the equivalent unenclosed share available
  regardless of which option is chosen.

### Second Rhapsody on the Red Cliff (1082, H6-E03)
- Range read: full (all three paragraphs, via Wikisource).
- Points: a winter return to the same cliffs; a solitary crane crosses the river; that night
  brings a dream of a Taoist who asks if the outing was enjoyable, then waking to find no one there.
- Reveals: landscape description turning, without comment, into a riddle about who is real.
- Advice angle: comfortable ending on an open question rather than a moral — a usable model for not
  forcing premature closure on an undecided choice.

### Calming the Waves: "Don't listen to the rain piercing the woods and striking the leaves" (translated) (1082, H6-E04)
- Range read: full (preface and all eight lines).
- Points: caught in rain near Shahu, companions scramble while Su Shi walks on unbothered; weather
  clears; looking back, the road held "no storm, and no clear sky either" (translated).
- Reveals: equanimity as indifference to the label "good" or "bad," not optimism the weather turns.
- Advice angle: guards against treating any one of O1–O4 as obviously the storm or the clear sky
  before it has actually happened.

### Self-Inscription on a Portrait at Jinshan (1101, H6-E06)
- Range read: full short piece, via a secondary transcription, not a critical edition.
- Points: seeing Su Shi's own portrait months before death, described as an unmoored boat in body
  and cold ash in mind, then naming the life's work as three exiles.
- Reveals: by the end, "accomplishment" is redefined around what was done inside setbacks.
- Advice angle: a late self-audit worth comparing against what a 25-year-old would guess in advance.

### Commonly attributed, not verified
- "If you bloom, the breeze will come" (translated) circulates on Chinese social media,
  sometimes captioned as Su Shi's. Not found in any source read this session; reads as a modern
  aphorism in classical dress. Not used as `[quoted]` anywhere in this dossier.

## H. Mental-model card

- Variables: can people absorb this change at this pace; is a public work actually finished, not
  just decreed; could a private draft become a public weapon; who depends on the next income.
- Heuristics: can this be absorbed at this pace, or does it only work forced through all at once?
  Who, locally, already knows this better than the court? If I lost this post tomorrow, what work
  would still be mine? Is this for one trusted reader, or will it travel beyond them?
- Red lines: will not call a policy a success when the people under it say otherwise, even at cost
  to the post held; will not stop teaching or writing because rank was stripped; will not call a
  public work done when it is only decreed.
