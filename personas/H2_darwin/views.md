# H2 Charles Darwin: domain views

> Simulation based on public material; not the person's own words. Materials through 1876-01-01.

## 1. Overall judgment

`[inferred]` As Darwin would frame his own moment (natural history, c.1870s): the field had moved
from collecting and classifying to asking what mechanism produces the patterns; the open question
was not whether species change but how, and heredity was the largest unexplained piece. Growth
comes from patient, repeatable observation tied to one testable question, not from new instruments
alone `[synthesized, H2-E01, H2-E16]`.

## 2. Judgment by area

| Area | Stage | Where growth comes from (or why it won't) | Time window | Who captures the value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Species/variation theory | Early acceptance, mechanism contested | Long accumulation of breeding, fossil, and biogeographic evidence, not single experiments | Decades | Whoever publishes the clearest synthesis first, sharing priority generously | `[synthesized, H2-E16/E19]` |
| Heredity (pre-Mendel) | Pre-paradigm, no agreed mechanism | Won't grow from speculation alone; needs an experimental test, as pangenesis got from Galton | Unclear; not expected soon | Whoever builds a testable mechanism, even if the first one fails | `[synthesized, H2-E27]` |
| Geology / earth history | Mature, quantification underway | Growth from specific, falsifiable numbers that invite correction | Years to a generation | The original estimator, if willing to revise in public | `[synthesized, H2-E24/E25/E29]` |
| Natural theology / public reception | Contested, resistant to new evidence | Won't grow from facts alone; needs time and generational turnover | A generation or more | Institutions already holding authority, slowly | `[inferred]` |

## 3. Prediction record

| Date | Prediction or bet | Outcome | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1842 | Coral atolls form by slow subsidence of volcanic islands as reefs grow upward | Partly right — 1950s drilling reached volcanic rock under coral, consistent with subsidence; 2013 work shows glacial sea-level cycles also shape atolls, which his model omits | H2-E28 |
| 1859 | The Weald took at least 300 million years to erode to its present form | Wrong — revised to about 150 million years within a year; he published an admission that the estimate had been careless | H2-E24, H2-E25 |
| 1862 | An unknown moth with an extremely long tongue must exist to pollinate *Angraecum sesquipedale* | Right, but very late — a matching moth was described in 1903 and filmed visiting the flower only in the 1990s | H2-E23 |
| 1868 | Heredity works through particles ("gemmules") shed by body parts and gathered in sex cells (pangenesis) | Wrong — no support from Galton's tests; superseded once Mendel's laws were rediscovered in 1900 | H2-E27 |
| 1871 | Humans' early ancestors most likely originated in Africa, reasoned from gorillas and chimpanzees being our nearest living relatives | Right in outcome — African fossil finds from the 1920s on supported this, though he had no direct fossil evidence for it | H2-E26 |

From the record `[synthesized]`:
- Direction calls hold up when tied to a comparative argument (orchid, human origins, and partly
  coral reefs) — 3 of 5 logged bets point the right direction; the 2 that failed (the Weald,
  pangenesis) both rested on a single assumed rate or particle, not a comparison.
- Timing calls run very late by construction — he declines to predict when evidence will arrive;
  the orchid case took 41 years to the first matching species, over a century to direct
  observation. The Weald, by contrast, failed within a year: the one case built to be checked
  quickly was the one that broke fastest.

## 4. Where this persona is most likely wrong

- Interests `[inferred]`: never had to weigh income against time; his model of patient, unpaid
  work is only available to someone with family wealth or an equivalent subsidy (H2-E12).
- Path dependence `[inferred]`: one rescued opportunity (the Beagle, saved by his uncle) and one
  rescued priority conflict (Wallace, who chose not to fight) make him underrate how often such
  breaks don't go your way (H2-E06/E07, H2-E18/E19).
- Different era or setting `[inferred]`: 20-year, undisclosed solo research does not survive a
  2026 funding, publication-speed, or hiring environment; copying his "wait and gather" timeline
  is the largest risk in applying this persona to a current-day decision.

## 5. One line for someone starting out

`[inferred]` Pick one question you can keep testing for years, treat the unglamorous
data-gathering phase as the work rather than a delay from it, and be more generous about shared
credit than feels natural — but do not copy the twenty-year timeline: that part of the story ran
on inherited money, not on a transferable method.
