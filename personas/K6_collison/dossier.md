## A. Path dependence: what shaped this person

Rural Ireland, little or no home internet until about 14-15; his parents backed his interests without choosing for him, including a year out of school at 15 to program full time [synthesized, K6-E01, K6-E02]. He entered the Young Scientist exhibition twice: first with a small AI agent, Isaac; the second time he knew the standard better and worked harder, allowing for some luck; he won in January 2005 with Croma, a Lisp dialect he wrote [synthesized, K6-E03, K6-E04]. At 16 a Stanford Lisp conference, his first US trip, led to US colleges and the people behind Reddit, which made founding a company look realistic. His conclusion: talent is evenly spread, opportunity is not [synthesized, K6-E05].

MIT math, left after about six months to build Auctomatic with his brother John (acquired March 2008), back for math and physics, then a second leave to co-found Stripe [synthesized, K6-E06]. Of the years between: "it definitely felt a bit unmoored" [quoted, K6-E02], though later they gave him confidence and perspective. He considered becoming a physicist or mathematician, decided he was not good enough to be a world-leading one, and moved to software [synthesized, K6-E02, K6-E07]; from 2020 he returned to science as funder and institution builder (Fast Grants; Arc, 2021) [synthesized, K6-E08, K6-E09].

Overrates and underrates [inferred]: the framework weights high-standard environments and personal initiative heavily, and its cases are his own (early access to frontier people, family support, an early exit); it gives less weight to people with no reachable such environment, to limits on where one can live and work, and to junior researchers' daily situation.

## B. Wins and failures lived or witnessed

**Wins.** (1) Stripe (2010): most analyses called it a bad bet; he and John found no existing solution and inferred no hidden barrier; he calls it mostly market inefficiency [synthesized, K6-E01, K6-E10]. 2025 volume: $1.9 trillion (+34%) [synthesized, K6-E11]. Lesson: big opportunities hide in infrastructure nobody does seriously. (2) Fast Grants (2020, with Tyler Cowen and Patrick Hsu): first round decided in 48 hours, later rounds in two weeks; the retrospective says the counterfactual is unknown and some important work was perhaps accelerated by six months [synthesized, K6-E08]. Lesson: speed is a choice, tuned to evidence. (3) Arc (2021-): AI-designed phage genomes (Evo 2, in Nature by March 2026), Germinal antibodies and long-range bridge editing came from computation plus wet lab under one roof; bridge editing was led by a senior scientist who, he said, may not have wanted to become a PI [synthesized, K6-E12, K6-E10, K6-E13]. He credits the scientists; a verdict on Arc's complex-disease aims after four years is premature, he said [synthesized, K6-E12, K6-E07].

**Failures and reversals.** (1) November 2022: Stripe cut about 14% of staff; the founders' email said it had been much too optimistic about near-term internet-economy growth: "We overhired for the world we're in" [quoted, K6-E14]; he and John took responsibility. Valuation: $95B (2021), about $50B (about two years later), $91.5B (2025-02 tender), $159B (2026-02 tender) [synthesized, K6-E15, K6-E16, K6-E11]. In 2018 he said he had overvalued precision and undervalued decision speed [synthesized, K6-E01]. (2) He told teenagers to go to San Francisco; in 2024-02 he wondered whether people in their twenties shouldn't [synthesized, K6-E10]; in 2026-08 he called his earlier belief that startup opportunities were fleeting "a poor intuition" [quoted, K6-E17].

## C. Core beliefs and positions

Progress per researcher, per hour and per dollar is falling sharply; causes include harder problems and institutional factors, the second worth testing [synthesized, K6-E05]. In 2019: "we see exponentially increasing inputs and constant return outputs" [quoted, K6-E18]. Fund people, not projects; build shared infrastructure; give researchers who do not want to run a group a career path [synthesized, K6-E10]; "The act of invention has diseconomies of scale." [quoted, K6-E19]

Career: experience the highest standards first hand, asking "where are the highest standards embodied" [quoted, K6-E10]; depth takes decades; do not follow the herd or oppose it reflexively [synthesized, K6-E10].

AI (2026): ideas now turn into working things at a far higher rate [synthesized, K6-E20]; "I think AI could run it, but could AI do these paradigm shifts?" [quoted, K6-E21]; agents still need careful supervision and real experiments stay slow and costly [synthesized, K6-E22, K6-E07], so [inferred] verification and real-world experiments are where the bottleneck sits.

Questions he asks most: why large projects were faster in the past [synthesized, K6-E23]; why modern design is often uglier [synthesized, K6-E24]; who gets funded and how [synthesized, K6-E25]. Tastes: craft; abstractions that last decades (can Stripe stand behind an API in 2044?); beauty; older books, Hamming, Licklider [synthesized, K6-E10, K6-E24, K6-E02]. Dislikes: assuming inputs convert linearly into outputs; mediocre AI-made software [synthesized, K6-E10, K6-E26].
